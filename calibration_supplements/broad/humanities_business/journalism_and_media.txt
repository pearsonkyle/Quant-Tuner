## Reporting, Sourcing, and Verification

Journalism is the practice of finding out what happened and telling people about it accurately, and
the discipline is almost entirely in the verification. Anyone can publish a claim; the value a
journalist adds is having checked it, and the methods for checking are specific and teachable rather
than a matter of instinct.

Sourcing is the first discipline. A source's reliability depends on their access to the information,
their independence from the interested parties, their track record, and their motive for speaking.
The most useful distinction is between a source who witnessed something and one who was told about
it, because the latter's account has passed through a filter that may not be visible. Documentary
evidence is preferred over testimony where it exists, since a document does not misremember or
reconstruct.

Corroboration means independent confirmation, and the word independent carries the weight. Two
sources who both heard it from the same person are one source. Two publications reporting the same
claim, where the second cited the first, are one publication. Circular sourcing is common enough
that tracing a claim back to its origin is a routine part of verifying it, and the exercise
frequently ends at a single unverified assertion that has been repeated until it acquired the
appearance of established fact.

  corroboration        independent confirmation

Q: Three separate outlets report the same claim. What does this establish?
A. The claim is verified by three independent sources
B. Nothing by itself — the outlets may all be reporting the same original source, so the claim must
   be traced to its origin to determine how many independent confirmations exist
C. The claim is false
D. Consensus among outlets is sufficient
Reasoning: Repetition across outlets is not corroboration when the outlets share a source. Tracing
the citation chain is what distinguishes three confirmations from one claim reported three times,
and the latter is far more common. Answer: B.

## Structure, Writing, and Presentation

News writing has a conventional structure that exists for practical reasons. The inverted pyramid
puts the most important information first and descends in order of significance, which originated in
the constraints of telegraph transmission and print layout and survives because readers leave at
unpredictable points. A reader who stops after two paragraphs should have the essential facts.

The lead sentence carries the most weight of any sentence in the piece. It should convey what
happened, to whom, and why it matters, without becoming a catalogue of every relevant detail. The
common failures are burying the significant fact several paragraphs down, and overloading the lead
until it conveys nothing clearly. Feature and narrative forms deliberately depart from this
structure, and the departure is effective precisely because the convention is strong.

Precision in language is the technical craft. Attribution should be exact about who said what, and
verbs of attribution carry connotations — said is neutral, claimed implies doubt, admitted implies
wrongdoing, and using them casually inserts a judgement the reporting may not support. Numbers
require context to mean anything: a percentage without a base, a total without a comparison, or a
change without a starting point are all technically true and practically uninformative.

## Data Journalism and Investigation

Data journalism applies analytical methods to reporting, and its failures are the familiar
statistical ones with a wider audience. Correlation reported as causation, a change in measurement
reported as a change in the phenomenon, a rate presented without its base, a comparison across
non-comparable categories — these are ordinary analytical errors that acquire real consequences when
published to millions.

The methodological discipline is straightforward and frequently skipped. Understand how the data was
collected and what it excludes, because administrative data records what an institution chose to
record. Check whether apparent changes are real or artifacts of definition changes, which are
extremely common in official statistics. Compare like with like, adjusting for population and for
composition. Consult someone who knows the domain, because the interpretation errors that matter are
usually domain-specific rather than statistical.

Investigative reporting is a longer-form practice with its own methods: document acquisition through
public records requests and leaks, systematic cross-referencing of records to find patterns
invisible in any single document, and building a factual case that will survive legal challenge.
Its defining characteristic is that the subject does not want the story published, which changes the
verification standard — every fact must be documented well enough to defend, and the standard is
closer to a legal one than to ordinary reporting.

  legal review         part of the process, not an obstacle to it
  source protection    technical and procedural; a promise that cannot be
                       withdrawn once made

## Media Economics and the Platform Environment

The economics of news changed fundamentally when distribution stopped being scarce. Print
publications bundled news with classifieds, listings, and advertising reach that had no substitute,
and that bundle funded reporting. Unbundling removed the subsidy: classifieds moved to dedicated
platforms, advertising moved to targeted networks with far better measurement, and the news itself
became one item competing for attention against everything else.

The revenue models that replaced it each shape the product. Advertising rewards attention volume,
which favours high-volume production, emotionally engaging framing, and headlines optimized for
clicks. Subscription rewards sustained value to a committed audience, which favours depth and
distinctiveness and narrows the audience. Philanthropic and public funding removes the commercial
pressure and introduces questions about independence from the funder. None is neutral, and reading
a publication's incentives explains a great deal about its output.

Platform intermediation added a further layer. When most readers arrive through algorithmic feeds and
search rather than directly, the platform's ranking determines what is seen, publishers optimize for
that ranking rather than for readers directly, and a change to the algorithm can eliminate a
publication's traffic without notice or recourse. The structural consequence is that publishers have
limited control over their own distribution, which is a weaker position than any previous
arrangement.

  unbundling           each component moving to a specialized platform
  the lost subsidy     reporting was funded by things that no longer need it
  advertising incentives  volume, engagement, and optimized headlines
  subscription incentives  depth and distinctiveness for a narrower audience
  funded models        commercial pressure removed, funder independence raised
  the reading skill    incentives explain much of the output

                       industry and remains unsolved

## Ethics and the Information Ecosystem

Journalism ethics is applied rather than abstract, and the recurring tensions are between publishing
what is true and minimizing harm, between the public interest and privacy, between independence and
access, and between speed and verification. These are genuine conflicts rather than problems with
correct answers, and the discipline lies in recognizing which conflict a decision involves and
reasoning explicitly about it.

The public interest test is the standard justification for intrusion, and it is frequently invoked
loosely. The question is whether the publication serves a substantial interest of the public rather
than merely interesting the public, and the distinction matters: exposing corruption in office is
the former, and a private matter that people are curious about is the latter. Being clear about
which one applies is what separates a defensible decision from a rationalization.

Independence is maintained by structure rather than by intention. Separation between the commercial
and editorial functions, disclosure of conflicts, and refusal of arrangements that create obligations
are procedural safeguards, and they exist because good intentions do not reliably survive commercial
pressure. Access journalism illustrates the failure mode: a reporter dependent on continued access to
powerful sources faces a real incentive not to publish what would end it, and the only defence is an
institution that supports the reporter through the consequences.
