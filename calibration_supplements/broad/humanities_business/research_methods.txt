## Question Formulation and Design

Research begins with a question, and the quality of the question bounds the quality of everything
after it. A well-formed research question is specific enough to be answerable with available
methods, sufficiently important that the answer matters, and open enough that both outcomes are
informative. A question whose answer is already known, or whose only interesting answer is one
particular result, is not research so much as demonstration.

The design follows from the question and from what can be manipulated. An experiment assigns the
condition of interest at random, which is what licenses causal conclusions, because randomization
makes the groups comparable on everything including factors nobody thought to measure. An
observational study takes conditions as they occur and must argue that the compared groups are
comparable, which is always weaker and is sometimes the only option available.

The distinction between exploratory and confirmatory work is the one most often blurred, and blurring
it is the source of a large fraction of unreliable published findings. Exploratory analysis generates
hypotheses by examining data flexibly; confirmatory analysis tests a pre-specified hypothesis with a
pre-specified procedure. Both are legitimate and they produce different kinds of evidence, and
presenting an exploratory finding as though it were confirmatory overstates it by a large and
unquantified amount.

Q: A study explores a dataset, finds an unexpected relationship, and reports it with a significance
test. What is wrong?
A. Nothing, if the test is correctly computed
B. The test assumes the hypothesis was specified before seeing the data; a relationship found by
   searching the data has a much higher chance of being noise, and the reported significance
   overstates the evidence by an amount that depends on how many comparisons were implicitly made
C. Exploratory analysis is invalid
D. The dataset is too small
Reasoning: The reference distribution for a significance test is the distribution of results under
the null for a pre-specified comparison. Searching a dataset makes many implicit comparisons, so the
most striking one is expected to look significant even under the null. The finding is a hypothesis
worth testing on new data, which is a legitimate and much weaker claim. Answer: B.

## Measurement

Measurement converts a concept into a number, and the gap between the concept and the number is
where most research error lives. Construct validity asks whether the measure captures the concept it
is meant to. Reliability asks whether repeated measurement gives consistent results. A measure can be
highly reliable and measure the wrong thing, which is the more dangerous combination because the
consistency inspires confidence.

Operationalization is the act of choosing the specific measure, and the choice constrains every
conclusion. A study of well-being measured by a survey instrument has studied responses to that
instrument, and generalizing to well-being requires an argument that the instrument tracks it.
Multiple measures of the same construct, showing consistent results, are much stronger evidence than
any single measure however carefully validated.

Measurement error attenuates relationships. Noise in a measured variable biases estimated
associations toward zero, so an observed weak relationship is compatible with a strong underlying
one measured badly. This means a null result from a noisy measure is much weaker evidence of absence
than the same result from a precise one, and reporting the measure's reliability is what allows a
reader to judge which situation they are in.

## Inference, Error, and Interpretation

Statistical inference generalizes from a sample to a population, and the machinery quantifies
uncertainty from sampling and from nothing else. Bias from selection, from measurement, from
attrition, and from analysis flexibility is not represented in a confidence interval at all, which
means a narrow interval indicates a large sample rather than a reliable result.

The errors of interpretation recur across fields and are worth naming explicitly. Statistical
significance is not practical importance; with a large enough sample, a trivial effect is
detectable. A non-significant result is not evidence of no effect, particularly when power was low.
Correlation supports causation only with a design that rules out the alternatives. And an effect
estimated in one population does not automatically apply to another, since the same intervention can
work differently where the context differs.

Multiplicity deserves particular attention because it is pervasive and easy to overlook. Examining
many outcomes, many subgroups, or many model specifications produces significant results by chance
at a rate proportional to the number examined, and the examination frequently happens informally
without being counted. The defences are pre-specification, correction for the comparisons actually
made, and reporting the full set examined rather than the subset that reached significance.

## Replication, Publication, and Synthesis

A single study is weak evidence regardless of its quality, because any one study can be wrong
through chance, through an undetected flaw, or through a context-specific result that does not
generalize. Replication is what converts a finding into knowledge, and the systematic replication
efforts in several fields have found that a substantial fraction of published findings do not
reproduce, which is a finding about the publication process rather than about the underlying
science.

Publication bias is the primary mechanism. Positive findings are more likely to be submitted, more
likely to be accepted, and more likely to be cited than null findings, so the published literature
overstates effects systematically. The magnitude can be large enough that a well-established effect
in the literature is absent when unpublished work is included, which is why registries of planned
studies and journals accepting results-blind submissions address the problem at its source rather
than correcting for it afterward.

Synthesis combines evidence across studies. A systematic review specifies its search and inclusion
criteria in advance so that the selection is reproducible rather than curated. Meta-analysis pools
effect estimates quantitatively, weighting by precision, and it inherits every bias in its inputs —
pooling biased studies produces a precise biased estimate, which is worse than an imprecise one
because it looks authoritative. Assessing publication bias and study quality is therefore part of
the method rather than an optional appendix.

## Communication and Integrity

Writing up research is part of the research rather than a report of it, because the discipline of
stating clearly what was done, what was found, and what follows exposes gaps that were invisible
while working. The structure that works is conventional for good reason: what question, what was
done, what was found, what it means, and what its limits are, in that order, with the limits stated
by the authors rather than left for readers to discover.

Reporting completeness is what makes work evaluable and reusable. The methods should permit
replication, which means stating what was actually done including the decisions made during the
work rather than the idealized procedure. Data and analysis code shared where possible remove
ambiguity entirely. Negative and unexpected results reported alongside the positive ones are what
prevent the literature from being a curated selection.

Research integrity spans a spectrum from fabrication at one end through selective reporting in the
middle to ordinary sloppiness at the other, and the middle is where most of the damage occurs
because it is common, rarely deliberate, and frequently not recognized as misconduct by those doing
it. Reporting the analysis that worked without mentioning the ones that did not, dropping
inconvenient observations for defensible-sounding reasons, and adjusting a hypothesis to match the
result are each individually explicable and collectively responsible for a literature less reliable
than it appears. The practices that prevent them are procedural — pre-specification, complete
reporting, and independent scrutiny — because relying on individual judgement under the incentives
that exist has been tried and has not worked.
