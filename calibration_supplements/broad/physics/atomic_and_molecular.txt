## Atomic Structure

The hydrogen atom is the reference case because it is exactly solvable, and every more complex atom
is described in language borrowed from it. A single electron bound to a proton has energy levels
depending only on the principal quantum number, spaced as the inverse square, converging to the
ionization limit. The degeneracy of states with the same principal quantum number but different
angular momentum is an accident of the Coulomb potential and is lifted in every other atom.

Multi-electron atoms require approximation because the electron-electron interaction has no closed
form. The central field approximation replaces it with an effective spherically symmetric potential
that each electron moves in, which restores the hydrogenic labelling while breaking the accidental
degeneracy: states of lower angular momentum penetrate closer to the nucleus, feel less screening
from the inner electrons, and lie lower in energy. This screening effect is what produces the
ordering of subshells and therefore the structure of the periodic table.

The Pauli exclusion principle fills those states, and Hund's rules determine the ground
configuration of a partially filled shell: maximize total spin first, then total orbital angular
momentum, then couple them according to whether the shell is less or more than half filled. The
spin rule follows from exchange — parallel spins keep electrons apart spatially and reduce their
repulsion — which is a genuinely quantum-mechanical origin for what looks like an empirical rule.

Atomic structure:
  hydrogen levels      energy proportional to minus one over n squared
  the accidental degeneracy  l-independence, unique to the Coulomb potential
  central field        an effective spherical potential per electron
  screening            inner electrons reduce the effective nuclear charge
  penetration          low-l states reach closer in and are bound more tightly
  subshell ordering    the consequence; the periodic table's structure
  Pauli principle      no two electrons in the same state
  Hund's rules         maximize S, then L, then J by shell filling
  exchange interaction  the origin of the spin rule; not a force but a
                       consequence of antisymmetry
  term symbols         the labelling scheme for a configuration's states
  LS coupling          valid for light atoms; spin-orbit weak compared to
                       electrostatic
  jj coupling          valid for heavy atoms; spin-orbit dominant
  the crossover        intermediate coupling; most of the middle table

## Spectra and Selection Rules

Atomic spectra are the primary observable and were the evidence that forced quantum mechanics. A
transition between two levels emits or absorbs a photon at the frequency corresponding to their
energy difference, and the resulting line spectrum is a fingerprint identifying the element
unambiguously — which is how the composition of stars is known.

Not every pair of levels is connected. Selection rules follow from the symmetry of the interaction
between the atom and the electromagnetic field, and for the dominant electric dipole term they
require the angular momentum to change by one unit and the parity to change. Transitions violating
these are not strictly impossible but proceed through much weaker higher-order terms, with rates
smaller by many orders of magnitude.

Forbidden transitions are astrophysically important precisely because they are slow. In a
laboratory gas, an atom in a metastable state is collisionally de-excited long before it can radiate
through a forbidden channel. In a nebula at a density of a few particles per cubic centimetre,
collisions are so rare that the forbidden transition is the dominant decay path, and the resulting
emission lines are among the brightest features of the spectrum. Lines that cannot be produced in a
laboratory therefore dominate the observation, which confused astronomers for decades before the
explanation was found.

Spectra and transitions:
  line spectrum        the element's fingerprint
  emission             downward transition, photon out
  absorption           upward transition, photon removed from a continuum
  electric dipole      the dominant interaction term
  selection rules      angular momentum changes by one, parity changes
  forbidden            allowed only at higher order; rates smaller by many orders
  metastable state     no allowed decay; long lived
  nebular lines        forbidden transitions dominating at very low density
  oscillator strength  the dimensionless transition probability
  lifetime             inverse of the total decay rate; sets the natural linewidth
  natural broadening   the uncertainty-principle linewidth; Lorentzian
  Doppler broadening   thermal motion; Gaussian, usually dominant in a hot gas
  pressure broadening  collisions interrupting the emission; increases with
                       density
  Voigt profile        the convolution of Lorentzian and Gaussian; what is
                       actually fitted

Q: An emission line in a nebula corresponds to a transition that cannot be produced in a
laboratory discharge tube. This is because:
A. The nebula contains an unknown element
B. The transition is forbidden and slow, so in the laboratory collisions de-excite the atom first;
   at nebular densities collisions are rare enough that the slow radiative decay dominates
C. The line is a measurement artifact
D. The physics differs in space
Reasoning: Forbidden transitions have very long radiative lifetimes. Whether they are observed
depends on whether collisional de-excitation is faster, which depends on density. The nebula's
extremely low density is the whole explanation. Answer: B.

## Fine, Hyperfine, and Atoms in Fields

The hydrogenic energy levels are a first approximation, and the corrections to them are where
atomic physics becomes precise. Fine structure arises from relativistic effects and from the
coupling of the electron's spin to the magnetic field it experiences in its own rest frame as it
orbits the nucleus. It splits levels by an amount scaling with the fine structure constant squared
relative to the gross structure, and it grows rapidly with nuclear charge.

Hyperfine structure arises from the interaction of the electron with the nuclear magnetic moment,
and it is smaller again by roughly the electron-to-proton mass ratio. It is nonetheless the basis
of the most consequential transitions in physics: the ground state hyperfine splitting of caesium
defines the second, and the corresponding splitting in atomic hydrogen produces the twenty-one
centimetre line that maps neutral hydrogen throughout the galaxy.

External fields split levels further and the splitting is diagnostic. The Zeeman effect splits by
magnetic field, with the splitting proportional to the field and to the magnetic quantum number,
which makes spectral line splitting a remote magnetometer — solar magnetic fields are measured this
way. The Stark effect splits by electric field, quadratically for most atoms and linearly for
hydrogen owing to its degeneracy, and it is the mechanism behind field ionization and the pressure
shifts in dense plasmas.

Corrections and external fields:
  fine structure       relativistic plus spin-orbit; scales as alpha squared
  spin-orbit coupling  the electron's spin in the field of its own orbit
  Lamb shift           a QED effect splitting levels degenerate in Dirac theory;
                       the measurement that launched quantum electrodynamics
  hyperfine structure  electron coupling to the nuclear moment
  the caesium standard  a hyperfine transition defines the SI second
  twenty-one centimetre  hydrogen's hyperfine line; maps galactic neutral gas
  isotope shift        mass and nuclear volume effects; measures nuclear radii
  Zeeman effect        magnetic splitting, linear in field
  anomalous Zeeman     the case with spin; requires the Landé g-factor
  Paschen-Back         strong field, where spin-orbit coupling is broken
  Stark effect         electric field splitting; quadratic generally, linear in
                       hydrogen
  field ionization     strong fields lowering the barrier until the electron
                       escapes
  Rydberg atoms        highly excited, enormous polarizability, extreme
                       sensitivity to fields; used as electrometers and as a
                       platform for strongly interacting quantum systems

## Molecular Structure

Molecules add nuclear degrees of freedom to the electronic problem, and the separation of scales
between them is what makes molecular physics tractable. The Born-Oppenheimer approximation treats
the nuclei as stationary while solving for the electrons, then uses the resulting electronic energy
as a potential surface on which the nuclei move. The justification is the mass ratio: electrons
respond essentially instantaneously to nuclear motion.

The resulting energy hierarchy is wide. Electronic transitions lie in the visible and ultraviolet,
vibrational transitions in the infrared, rotational transitions in the microwave, and each is
separated from the next by roughly two orders of magnitude. This separation is why infrared
spectroscopy identifies functional groups — a vibrational frequency depends mainly on the local
bond and its reduced mass, so it transfers between molecules — and why microwave spectroscopy
determines molecular geometry, since rotational constants are inverse moments of inertia.

Bonding itself is described by molecular orbital theory, in which atomic orbitals combine into
bonding and antibonding combinations, and the bond order follows from how the electrons fill them.
The competing valence bond picture localizes electrons into pairs and is more chemically intuitive;
both are approximations to the same underlying problem and each is more convenient for different
questions.

Molecular structure:
  Born-Oppenheimer     separate nuclear and electronic motion by mass ratio
  potential energy surface  electronic energy as a function of nuclear positions
  the energy hierarchy  electronic, vibrational, rotational, separated by roughly
                       two orders of magnitude each
  molecular orbitals   atomic orbitals combining into bonding and antibonding
  bond order           net bonding electron pairs
  vibrational modes    3N minus 6 for a nonlinear molecule, minus 5 for linear
  harmonic approximation  good near the minimum; anharmonicity matters for
                       overtones and dissociation
  infrared activity    requires a changing dipole moment
  Raman activity       requires a changing polarizability; complementary to
                       infrared
  rotational constants  inverse moments of inertia; determine geometry
  selection rules      vibrational transitions change v by one in the harmonic
                       limit; rotational by one
  Franck-Condon        electronic transitions are vertical on the nuclear
                       coordinate; the intensity pattern reflects wavefunction
                       overlap
  dissociation         the potential surface flattening; the asymptote gives the
                       bond energy

## Laser Cooling, Trapping, and Precision Measurement

Laser cooling exploits the fact that a photon carries momentum. An atom moving toward a laser tuned
slightly below its resonance sees the light Doppler-shifted into resonance, absorbs preferentially
from that direction, and re-emits in a random direction, so the momentum transfer averages to a
force opposing motion. Six beams along three axes produce a viscous damping in all directions,
cooling a gas to the microkelvin range and below.

Trapping requires a position-dependent force in addition, supplied by a magnetic field gradient
that Zeeman-shifts the atoms into resonance more strongly the further they are from the centre.
Combined with the cooling beams this makes a magneto-optical trap, the workhorse of the field.
Further cooling by evaporation — removing the most energetic atoms and letting the rest
re-thermalize — reaches the nanokelvin regime where quantum degeneracy appears and a
Bose-Einstein condensate forms.

The payoff is precision. A cold atom moves slowly, so it can be interrogated for a long time, and
the linewidth of a measurement is inversely proportional to the interrogation time. Optical clocks
based on a narrow transition in a trapped ion or in neutral atoms held in an optical lattice reach
fractional frequency uncertainties near one part in ten to the eighteenth, which is sensitive
enough that the gravitational redshift over a height difference of a centimetre is measurable.

  radiation pressure   photon momentum transfer; the basic force
  Doppler cooling      red-detuned beams; velocity-dependent damping
  Doppler limit        set by the random-recoil heating from spontaneous emission
  sub-Doppler          polarization gradient mechanisms beating the naive limit
  magneto-optical trap  cooling beams plus a field gradient
  optical molasses     damping without confinement
  optical tweezers     a focused beam trapping by the dipole force; single-atom
                       control
  optical lattice      a standing wave making a periodic potential; a clean
                       platform for condensed matter analogues
  evaporative cooling  remove the hot tail, re-thermalize; the route to
                       degeneracy
  Bose-Einstein condensate  macroscopic occupation of the ground state
  degenerate Fermi gas  the fermionic counterpart; Pauli blocking observable
  atomic clock         a transition frequency as the time standard
  optical clock        an optical rather than microwave transition; the higher
                       frequency buys precision directly
  the redshift measurement  optical clocks resolve gravitational time dilation
                       over centimetres of height
  atom interferometry  splitting and recombining matter waves; measures
                       accelerations, rotations, and the gravitational constant
  the broader point    atomic physics supplies the most precise measurements in
                       science, and those measurements are how the constancy of
                       fundamental constants, the existence of new forces, and
                       the limits of the standard model are tested
