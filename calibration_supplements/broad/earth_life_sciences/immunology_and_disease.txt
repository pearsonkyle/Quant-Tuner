## Innate Immunity

The immune system's central problem is discriminating self from non-self, and doing so against
pathogens that evolve far faster than their hosts. The solution is two layered systems with
complementary properties: an innate system that responds immediately using genetically encoded
recognition of broadly conserved features, and an adaptive system that responds slowly with
recognition generated at random and selected for specificity.

Barriers are the first line and are frequently omitted from accounts of immunity despite doing
most of the work. Intact skin is impermeable to nearly everything. Mucus traps particles and
cilia move them out. Stomach acid destroys most ingested organisms. Antimicrobial peptides are
secreted onto epithelial surfaces. And the commensal microbiota occupies the niches a pathogen
would need, which is why disrupting it with antibiotics permits opportunistic infections that the
competition otherwise prevents.

Innate recognition uses pattern recognition receptors detecting molecular features common to
classes of pathogen and absent from the host — bacterial cell wall components, double-stranded
RNA, flagellin, unmethylated CpG DNA. These features are conserved because they are essential to
the pathogen, so they cannot be discarded to evade detection, which is what makes a
genetically fixed receptor set viable against evolving organisms.

Innate immunity:
  barriers             skin, mucus, cilia, acid, antimicrobial peptides,
                       commensal competition
  pattern recognition  receptors for conserved pathogen features
  PAMPs                pathogen-associated molecular patterns; essential and
                       therefore not discardable
  DAMPs                damage-associated patterns; signal tissue injury
  Toll-like receptors  the best-characterized family; each detects a distinct
                       pattern class
  inflammation         vasodilation, permeability, and leukocyte recruitment;
                       heat, redness, swelling, pain
  cytokines            signalling proteins coordinating the response

                       lyses; three activation pathways converging on one
  phagocytes           neutrophils and macrophages; engulf and destroy
  neutrophils          first responders, short-lived, abundant
  macrophages          tissue-resident, phagocytic, and the principal antigen
                       presenters bridging to adaptive immunity
  natural killer cells  kill cells lacking normal surface markers; the
                       counter to pathogens that downregulate them to hide from
                       T cells
  interferons          antiviral state induction in neighbouring cells

Natural killer cells illustrate an elegant solution to an evasion strategy. Cytotoxic T cells
recognize infected cells by the antigen displayed on class I major histocompatibility molecules,
so a virus that downregulates those molecules becomes invisible to them. Natural killer cells
detect the absence of those molecules and kill on that basis, so the evasion strategy triggers a
different killing mechanism. The pathogen faces a bind: display the molecules and be killed by T
cells, or hide them and be killed by natural killer cells.

Q: A virus evolves to suppress display of class I MHC molecules on infected cells, evading
cytotoxic T cells. The immune system's counter is:
A. Antibodies, which do not require MHC
B. Natural killer cells, which detect the absence of MHC and kill on that basis
C. Complement
D. Fever
Reasoning: The missing-self hypothesis describes exactly this: natural killer cells are
inhibited by normal MHC display and activated in its absence, so downregulation that evades T
cells triggers them instead. This complementary pairing is why the evasion strategy is
constrained rather than decisive. Answer: B.

## Adaptive Immunity and Antigen Receptors

Adaptive immunity generates receptor diversity by somatic recombination rather than by encoding
each receptor in the genome. Gene segments are randomly selected and joined, with additional
random nucleotides inserted at the junctions, producing an enormous repertoire from a modest
number of segments — estimates exceed ten to the eleventh distinct receptors from a few hundred
gene segments. Each lymphocyte expresses one receptor specificity, so the repertoire is
distributed across the cell population.

Because the receptors are generated randomly, many recognize self, and eliminating those is a
central task. Central tolerance removes strongly self-reactive lymphocytes during development in
the thymus and bone marrow. Peripheral tolerance handles those that escape, through anergy,
suppression by regulatory T cells, and deletion. Failure of these mechanisms produces
autoimmunity, and the fact that tolerance works as well as it does against a randomly generated
repertoire is remarkable.

Clonal selection is the organizing principle. Each lymphocyte bears one specificity, an antigen
binds the small number of cells whose receptors fit, and those cells proliferate into a large
clone. This explains the response's timing directly: the primary response is slow because it
requires expanding a rare clone from a handful of cells, and the secondary response is fast
because memory cells from the first expansion are already numerous.

Adaptive immunity:
  somatic recombination  random assembly of gene segments; enormous diversity
                       from few genes
  junctional diversity  random nucleotides at the joins; further diversity
  clonal selection      antigen binds the fitting cells; those proliferate
  central tolerance    self-reactive cells deleted during development
  peripheral tolerance  anergy, suppression, deletion for escapees
  regulatory T cells   suppress responses; their failure causes autoimmunity
  B cells              produce antibodies; recognize antigen directly in its
                       native conformation
  T cells              recognize peptide fragments presented on MHC; cannot see
                       free antigen
  helper T cells       coordinate; the cells HIV depletes, which is why its
                       consequences are so broad
  cytotoxic T cells    kill infected cells displaying foreign peptide on class I
  MHC class I          on all nucleated cells; presents cytosolic peptides,
                       reporting what the cell is making
  MHC class II         on antigen presenting cells; presents engulfed material
  antigen presentation  the requirement that T cells see processed peptide rather
                       than intact antigen
  affinity maturation  somatic hypermutation and selection in germinal centres;
                       antibody affinity improves over weeks
  class switching      the same specificity moved to a different antibody class,
                       changing the effector function while keeping the target

Affinity maturation deserves emphasis because it is evolution operating within an individual on
a timescale of weeks. B cells responding to an antigen enter germinal centres where their
antibody genes undergo a mutation rate far above the background, and those whose mutations improve
binding are selected to survive and proliferate while the rest die. The result is that antibody
affinity rises by orders of magnitude over the course of a response, and it is why a secondary
response produces not merely more antibody but substantially better antibody.

## Memory, Vaccination, and Pathogens

Immunological memory is the property vaccination exploits, and its mechanism is the persistence
of expanded antigen-specific clones after an infection resolves. Memory B cells and T cells are
present at far higher frequency than the original naive precursors, are pre-selected for
affinity, and respond faster with less costimulation required. The secondary response therefore
begins within days rather than weeks and produces higher-affinity antibody of a more effective
class.

Vaccines induce this memory without the disease, and the platforms differ in what they present
and therefore in what response they elicit. Live attenuated vaccines replicate and produce strong
and durable responses of both antibody and cellular type, and cannot be given to
immunocompromised individuals. Inactivated and subunit vaccines are safe in anyone and typically
produce weaker, antibody-dominant responses requiring adjuvants and boosters. Nucleic acid
vaccines deliver instructions for the host to produce the antigen, which yields both antibody and
cellular responses and permits rapid design once a sequence is known.

Pathogens evade immunity by identifiable strategies, and knowing them explains why some
infections are controlled and others are not. Antigenic variation changes the recognized surface
so that existing immunity does not apply, which is why influenza requires annual reformulation.
Latency hides in a non-replicating state where there is nothing to detect. Intracellular
residence avoids antibodies entirely. Immunosuppression attacks the response directly. And
molecular mimicry resembles host molecules, exploiting tolerance.

Memory, vaccines, and evasion:
  memory cells         expanded, pre-selected, faster and lower-threshold
  primary response     slow; requires clonal expansion from rare precursors
  secondary response   fast, higher affinity, better class
  live attenuated      strong and durable; contraindicated in immunosuppression
  inactivated          safe, weaker, needs adjuvant and boosting
  subunit and conjugate  purified components; conjugation to a carrier protein
                       recruits T cell help, which is what makes polysaccharide
                       vaccines work in young children
  viral vector         delivers the antigen gene; pre-existing vector immunity can
                       blunt it
  mRNA                 host produces the antigen; rapid design, cellular and
                       antibody responses
  adjuvant             enhances the response by providing innate stimulation
  correlate of protection  a measurable marker predicting protection; where it is
                       known, licensure can rely on it
  herd immunity        indirect protection when transmission is interrupted; the
                       threshold rises with transmissibility
  antigenic drift      gradual mutation; annual influenza reformulation
  antigenic shift      reassortment producing a novel subtype; pandemic potential
  latency              non-replicating persistence; herpesviruses, tuberculosis
  intracellular        avoids antibodies; requires cellular immunity
  original antigenic sin  the first exposure biases later responses toward the

Original antigenic sin deserves note because it complicates both vaccination and the
interpretation of immunity. The immune system responding to a variant of a previously encountered
pathogen preferentially recalls the memory response to the original rather than generating a new
response to the variant's distinct features. This is efficient when the variants are similar and
counterproductive when the response is directed at conserved regions that are no longer the
important targets, and it is part of why influenza vaccine effectiveness depends on an
individual's exposure history and not only on the match between the vaccine and the circulating
strain.

## Immune Dysfunction

Immune dysfunction divides into insufficient response, excessive response, and misdirected
response, and the categories have different clinical presentations and mechanisms.

Immunodeficiency may be primary, arising from a genetic defect in a component, or secondary,
arising from infection, malnutrition, malignancy, or treatment. The pattern of infections
identifies the defective component: recurrent bacterial infections with encapsulated organisms
suggest an antibody or complement defect, persistent viral and fungal infections suggest a
cellular defect, and recurrent abscesses suggest a phagocyte defect. This is diagnostically
useful because the infection pattern points at the mechanism.

Hypersensitivity is an excessive or inappropriate response, classified into four types by
mechanism. Type I is immediate, mediated by IgE bound to mast cells, and produces allergy and
anaphylaxis within minutes. Type II is antibody directed against cell surface antigens. Type III
is immune complex deposition causing inflammation where the complexes lodge. Type IV is cell
mediated and delayed by a day or more, which is contact dermatitis and the tuberculin reaction.

Autoimmunity is a response directed at self, arising from a failure of tolerance. Contributing
factors include genetic susceptibility, particularly in the major histocompatibility genes,
environmental triggers, and molecular mimicry where a pathogen antigen resembles a self antigen
closely enough that the response cross-reacts. Autoimmune conditions are substantially more
common in women, and the reasons are not fully established.

  primary immunodeficiency  genetic; the infection pattern identifies the
                       component
  secondary            infection, malnutrition, malignancy, immunosuppression
  antibody defect      recurrent encapsulated bacterial infection
  cellular defect      persistent viral and fungal infection
  phagocyte defect     recurrent abscesses
  complement defect    recurrent neisserial infection specifically
  type I hypersensitivity  IgE and mast cells; minutes; allergy and anaphylaxis
  type II              antibody against cell surfaces
  type III             immune complex deposition
  type IV              cell mediated; delayed by a day or more
  autoimmunity         tolerance failure; genetic and environmental contributions
  molecular mimicry    pathogen antigen resembling self; cross-reactive response
  transplant rejection  the immune response to non-self MHC; matching and
                       immunosuppression are the responses
  graft versus host    transplanted immune cells attacking the recipient
  cytokine storm       dysregulated inflammatory amplification; the mechanism of
                       severe outcomes in several infections
  immune privilege     sites with restricted immune access; eye, brain, testis;

The distinction between damage caused by a pathogen and damage caused by the response to it is
worth stating because it determines treatment. In several severe infections the tissue damage is
substantially immune-mediated rather than directly pathogen-mediated, so treatment includes
modulating the response as well as targeting the organism. This is why corticosteroids improve
outcomes in some severe infections and worsen them in others, and why the timing matters — early
suppression impairs clearance while late suppression limits the damage from an already
dysregulated response.

## Epidemiology and Control

Infectious disease epidemiology quantifies transmission, and the basic reproduction number is
the central parameter: the average number of secondary cases produced by one case in a fully
susceptible population. Above one an epidemic grows and below one it declines, and the effective
reproduction number accounts for existing immunity and control measures, which is what determines
the trajectory in practice.

The herd immunity threshold follows directly. Transmission is interrupted when the fraction
immune exceeds one minus the reciprocal of the basic reproduction number, so a pathogen with a
reproduction number of two requires half the population immune and one with twelve requires
about 92 percent. This is why measles, being extremely transmissible, requires such high
vaccination coverage and why small declines in coverage produce outbreaks.

Several caveats apply to that calculation and are frequently omitted. It assumes homogeneous
mixing, and real contact is highly structured, so clusters of low coverage sustain transmission
even where the aggregate exceeds the threshold. It assumes immunity blocks transmission rather
than only disease, which differs between pathogens and vaccines. And it assumes immunity is
durable, which for some pathogens it is not.

Epidemiology and control:
  basic reproduction number  secondary cases from one case in a fully susceptible
                       population
  effective reproduction number  accounting for immunity and control; the
                       quantity that determines the trajectory
  herd immunity threshold  1 minus 1/R0; assumes homogeneous mixing
  heterogeneous mixing  clusters of susceptibility sustain transmission despite
                       adequate aggregate coverage
  serial interval      time between successive cases; sets how fast interventions
                       must act
  incubation period    infection to symptoms; pre-symptomatic transmission makes
                       symptom-based control ineffective
  latent period        infection to infectiousness; the relation between latent
                       and incubation periods determines whether isolation on
                       symptoms can work
  case fatality ratio  deaths among diagnosed cases; depends on testing intensity
  infection fatality ratio  deaths among all infections; the epidemiologically
                       meaningful figure and much harder to measure
  contact tracing      effective when the serial interval is long and

  surveillance         case-based, syndromic, wastewater, genomic; each detects

Antimicrobial resistance deserves the closing emphasis because it is evolution operating on a
timescale that policy must accommodate. Resistance genes generally predate the clinical use of the
drug — many are found in environmental organisms and in preserved specimens from before
antibiotics existed — so the drug does not create resistance but selects for organisms already
carrying it. The rate at which resistance spreads therefore depends on the selective pressure,
which is the total volume of use across human medicine, agriculture, and the environment. The
mitigations follow: reduce unnecessary use, complete courses appropriately for the specific
infection rather than by a general rule, use narrow-spectrum agents where the organism is known,
control transmission of resistant organisms, and develop new agents — with the difficulty that
the economics of antibiotic development are poor precisely because a successful new agent should
be reserved rather than sold widely.
