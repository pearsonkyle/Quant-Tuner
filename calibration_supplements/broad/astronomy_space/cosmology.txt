## Expansion and Cosmological Models

Cosmology applies general relativity to the universe as a whole, and it becomes tractable
because of an assumption that observation supports: on scales above a few hundred million
light-years, the universe is homogeneous and isotropic — the same everywhere and in every
direction. This cosmological principle reduces the field equations to a pair of ordinary
differential equations for a single scale factor describing how distances grow with time.

Hubble's observation that galaxies recede with velocity proportional to distance is the
foundational measurement. The relation is not a velocity through space but an expansion of
space itself, which matters for interpretation: there is no centre from which expansion
proceeds, every observer sees the same recession pattern, and the recession speed of
sufficiently distant objects exceeds light without violating relativity, because nothing is
moving through space faster than light.

Cosmological redshift follows from the same picture and is distinct from a Doppler shift. As
light travels, space expands, and the wavelength stretches proportionally. The redshift
therefore measures the ratio of the scale factor now to the scale factor when the light was
emitted, which is why redshift is a direct measure of the epoch of emission and is used as the
standard cosmological time coordinate.

Foundational relations:
  cosmological principle  homogeneous and isotropic on large scales
  Hubble's law            v = H_0 d;  H_0 ≈ 67-73 km/s/Mpc
  scale factor            a(t);  a = 1 today
  redshift                1 + z = 1/a at emission
  Hubble time             1/H_0 ≈ 14 Gyr; a rough age estimate
  Friedmann equations     expansion rate from the energy content and curvature
  critical density        the density giving flat geometry; ~9e-27 kg/m^3
  density parameter       Omega = density / critical density
  flat universe           Omega_total = 1; measured to be flat within error
  deceleration            expansion slows under matter, accelerates under dark
                          energy
  comoving distance       distance factoring out the expansion; constant for
                          objects moving with the flow
  particle horizon        the maximum distance from which light could have reached
                          us; ~46 Gly comoving, larger than c times the age because
                          space expanded during transit

The observable universe's radius exceeding the speed of light times its age is a point that
generates persistent confusion and has a straightforward explanation. Light emitted 13.8
billion years ago has travelled for that duration, and the space it crossed has expanded while
it travelled, so the emitting material is now about 46 billion light-years away. Nothing
travelled faster than light; the intervening distance grew after the light passed through it.

Q: A galaxy is observed at redshift z = 3. The universe has expanded by what factor since
that light was emitted?
A. 3   B. 4   C. 1/3   D. 9
Reasoning: The relation is 1 + z = a_now / a_then, so 1 + 3 = 4. The universe was one quarter
of its present size, and all wavelengths have been stretched by a factor of four. Answer: B.

## The Hot Big Bang and Nucleosynthesis

Running the expansion backward implies that the universe was denser and hotter in the past,
and the hot Big Bang model traces the consequences. At early times the universe was a dense
plasma in thermal equilibrium, and as it expanded and cooled, successive processes fell out of
equilibrium and left relics that are observable today. The model's strength is that these
relics are quantitative predictions rather than post-hoc explanations.

The sequence of epochs is set by the temperature at which each interaction becomes too slow
relative to the expansion to maintain equilibrium. Within the first second, the weak
interactions maintaining the neutron-to-proton ratio froze out, fixing that ratio at roughly
one to six. Between about one second and a few minutes, nuclear reactions built light
elements. About 380,000 years later, the temperature fell enough for electrons and nuclei to
combine into neutral atoms, at which point photons decoupled and travelled freely — producing
the microwave background.

Big Bang nucleosynthesis produced the light elements and is one of the model's strongest
quantitative successes. Essentially all the helium-4 in the universe, about 24 percent by
mass, was made in those few minutes, along with trace deuterium, helium-3, and lithium-7. The
predicted abundances depend on a single free parameter — the ratio of baryons to photons — and
they span nine orders of magnitude in abundance while agreeing with observation. Deuterium is
the most sensitive probe, since it is destroyed rather than made in stars, so its primordial
abundance is inferred from the least-processed gas available and gives a tight constraint on
the baryon density.

The hot Big Bang timeline:
  < 1e-32 s        inflation (proposed); exponential expansion
  1e-6 s           quarks bind into protons and neutrons
  1 s              neutrinos decouple; the neutron-proton ratio freezes
  1-200 s          nucleosynthesis; helium-4, deuterium, helium-3, lithium-7
  ~50,000 yr       matter-radiation equality; structure growth can begin
  ~380,000 yr      recombination; photons decouple, the CMB is released
  ~150-1000 Myr    first stars; reionization of the intergalactic medium
  ~1 Gyr           galaxies assembling
  ~9 Gyr           dark energy begins to dominate; expansion accelerates
  13.8 Gyr         present
  predicted He-4   ~24% by mass; observed, and the agreement is a major success
  deuterium        the most sensitive baryon-density probe; destroyed by stars
  lithium-7        predicted abundance exceeds observation by about a factor of
                   three; an unresolved discrepancy

The lithium problem deserves mention because it is an honest anomaly. Predicted primordial
lithium-7 exceeds what is measured in the oldest stars by roughly a factor of three, a
discrepancy well outside the quoted uncertainties. Proposed explanations include stellar
processes depleting lithium in those stars, unknown nuclear reaction rates, and new physics.
It is a genuine unresolved problem in a model that otherwise fits the light element abundances
across nine orders of magnitude, which is the appropriate framing — a specific outstanding
discrepancy within a heavily confirmed framework.

## The Cosmic Microwave Background

The microwave background is relic radiation from recombination, when the universe became
transparent. Its discovery in 1965 as an unexplained excess noise in a radio antenna
decisively favoured the hot Big Bang over the steady-state alternative, which had no mechanism
to produce a thermal background.

Its spectrum is the most perfect blackbody ever measured, matching a temperature of 2.7255
kelvin with deviations below a part in ten thousand. That precision is itself informative: it
means the early universe was in thermal equilibrium and that no substantial energy was
injected afterward, which constrains a range of exotic processes.

The temperature fluctuations are the payload. After removing the dipole caused by our own
motion, the residual variations are about one part in a hundred thousand, and their
statistical pattern encodes the composition and geometry of the universe. The fluctuations
arose from acoustic oscillations in the pre-recombination plasma: gravity pulled matter into
overdense regions, radiation pressure pushed back, and the resulting standing waves were
frozen in when photons decoupled.

The power spectrum of these fluctuations — the amplitude as a function of angular scale —
shows a series of peaks whose positions and relative heights are precise diagnostics. The
first peak's angular position measures the geometry, because it corresponds to a known
physical size at a known distance and the observed angle depends on the curvature of space
between. Its measurement establishes that the universe is flat to within a fraction of a
percent. The ratio of odd to even peak heights measures the baryon density, since baryons add
inertia to the oscillation and enhance compressions relative to rarefactions. The third peak
constrains the dark matter density.

CMB observables:
  temperature           2.7255 K; blackbody to better than 1 part in 10^4
  dipole                ~3.4 mK; our motion at ~370 km/s relative to the CMB frame
  fluctuations          ~1 part in 10^5 after dipole removal
  angular power spectrum  amplitude versus angular scale; the primary dataset
  first peak position   measures spatial curvature; the universe is flat
  odd/even peak ratio   measures baryon density
  third peak            constrains dark matter density
  damping tail          small-scale suppression from the finite thickness of the
                        last scattering surface
  polarization, E-mode  from density perturbations; measured, and consistent
  polarization, B-mode  primordial ones would be evidence for inflationary
                        gravitational waves; not yet detected, and foreground dust
                        has produced at least one retracted claim
  lensing               the CMB is gravitationally lensed by intervening structure;
                        measurable and constrains the matter distribution

The consistency between independent probes is what makes the concordance model credible.
The baryon density inferred from CMB peak ratios agrees with the value inferred from
primordial deuterium, which is a completely independent measurement of a completely different
phenomenon at a completely different epoch. The matter density from the CMB agrees with that
from galaxy clustering and from cluster abundances. Agreement among methods with unrelated
systematics is a far stronger argument than the precision of any single one.

## Structure Formation, Dark Matter, and Dark Energy

Structure grew from the tiny density fluctuations visible in the CMB through gravitational
instability: overdense regions attract more matter, becoming more overdense. The growth rate
is set by the competition between gravity and expansion, and it is slow — density contrast
grows only linearly with the scale factor during matter domination, which is why the universe
needed billions of years to form galaxies from fluctuations of one part in a hundred
thousand.

That timeline is impossible without dark matter, and this is one of the strongest arguments
for it. Ordinary matter was coupled to radiation until recombination, so its fluctuations
could not grow during that entire period — radiation pressure erased them. Dark matter, not
interacting with radiation, began growing much earlier, and by recombination it had already
formed the potential wells into which ordinary matter fell. Without that head start, the
structure we observe could not have formed from CMB-level fluctuations in the available time.

The evidence for dark matter comes from several independent phenomena at different scales.
Galaxy rotation curves stay flat far beyond the visible disc rather than declining as Kepler's
laws would require. Velocity dispersions in galaxy clusters imply far more mass than the
luminous content. Gravitational lensing maps mass directly and finds it exceeds the visible.
The CMB peak structure requires a non-baryonic matter component. And the Bullet Cluster, where
two clusters have collided, shows the lensing mass separated from the X-ray-emitting gas —
which is what collisionless dark matter would do while the gas, being collisional, was slowed
in the collision.

Dark sector evidence and properties:
  rotation curves        flat far beyond the luminous disc
  cluster dynamics       velocity dispersions imply excess mass
  gravitational lensing  maps mass directly; exceeds visible matter
  CMB peaks              require non-baryonic matter
  structure formation    growth timeline is impossible without an early-growing
                         component
  Bullet Cluster         lensing mass separated from collisional gas; strong
                         evidence for a collisionless component
  candidates             WIMPs, axions, sterile neutrinos, primordial black holes;
                         direct detection experiments have set stringent limits
                         without a confirmed signal
  MOND                   modified gravity; fits galaxy rotation curves well and
                         struggles with clusters, the Bullet Cluster, and the CMB
  dark energy            discovered 1998 from Type Ia supernovae appearing fainter
                         than a decelerating universe predicts
  equation of state      w = -1 for a cosmological constant; measurements are
                         consistent with -1 within errors
  composition            ~5% baryons, ~27% dark matter, ~68% dark energy

Dark energy's problem is theoretical rather than observational. A cosmological constant is
the simplest explanation, corresponds to a constant energy density of the vacuum, and fits the
data. Quantum field theory's naive estimate of the vacuum energy exceeds the observed value by
something between fifty and a hundred and twenty orders of magnitude depending on the cutoff
assumed, which is the largest discrepancy between prediction and observation in physics. That
the observed value is small and nonzero rather than either zero or enormous is unexplained,
and the coincidence that it became dominant at roughly the present epoch — rather than long
before galaxies formed or long after — is an additional puzzle without an accepted resolution.

## Inflation and Open Questions

Inflation proposes a brief period of exponential expansion in the first fraction of a second,
and it was introduced to solve three problems in the standard hot Big Bang. The horizon
problem is that regions of the CMB separated by more than about a degree could never have been
in causal contact, yet they have the same temperature to a part in a hundred thousand. The
flatness problem is that the density parameter's deviation from one grows with time, so its
being close to one today requires it to have been extraordinarily close to one initially. The
monopole problem is that grand unified theories predict magnetic monopoles which are not
observed.

Exponential expansion addresses all three with one mechanism. A region small enough to have
been in causal contact is inflated to encompass the entire observable universe, explaining the
uniformity. Any curvature is stretched flat, as the surface of an inflating balloon becomes
locally flatter. And any pre-existing relics are diluted to undetectable density.

Inflation's most consequential feature is one it was not designed to provide: quantum
fluctuations during the inflationary period are stretched to macroscopic scales and become the
seed density perturbations from which all structure grew. This makes the specific statistical
properties of the CMB fluctuations a prediction — nearly scale-invariant, nearly Gaussian,
with a slight tilt — and those predictions have been confirmed. The definitive test would be
detection of primordial gravitational waves through their B-mode polarization signature, which
has not been achieved and would strongly constrain the energy scale of inflation.

The Hubble tension is the most active current disagreement and deserves the closing word.
Measurements of the expansion rate from the CMB, extrapolated forward using the standard
model, give roughly 67 kilometres per second per megaparsec. Direct measurements using the
local distance ladder — Cepheids calibrating Type Ia supernovae — give roughly 73. The
discrepancy is several times the quoted uncertainties, both methods have been scrutinized
extensively, and independent methods have not decisively resolved it. Either one measurement
harbours an unrecognized systematic, or the standard model requires modification in a way that
changes the extrapolation between the early and late universe. Which of these is true is
genuinely undetermined, and it is the clearest current example of a precision measurement
disagreement that may or may not indicate new physics.
