## Randomization Infrastructure

An experimentation platform exists to make causal measurement routine, and most of its value is in
the infrastructure rather than in the statistics. Assignment must be deterministic given the unit
and the experiment, so that a user sees a consistent experience across sessions and devices; it must
be independent across concurrent experiments, so that overlapping tests do not confound each other;
and it must be logged at the point of assignment, so that analysis uses exactly the population that
was assigned.

Hashing the unit identifier together with an experiment-specific salt achieves the first two
properties. Determinism follows from the hash being a pure function; independence follows from
different salts producing uncorrelated partitions. Reusing a salt across experiments, or bucketing by
a value correlated with the outcome, produces correlated assignment that is difficult to detect
after the fact and that invalidates everything downstream.

The randomization unit is a design choice with consequences. Randomizing by user gives clean
independence and requires stable identification. Randomizing by session is easier and permits the
same user to see both variants, which contaminates any effect that persists across sessions.
Randomizing by cluster — an account, a household, a geography — is required when units within the
cluster influence each other, and it substantially reduces effective sample size, which must be
accounted for in the power calculation rather than discovered afterward.

Q: An experiment intended to split traffic evenly shows fifty-two percent in treatment. The result
looks positive and significant. What should be done?
A. Ship it; the imbalance is small
B. Investigate the imbalance before interpreting anything — a ratio mismatch indicates a systematic
   problem in assignment or logging that usually also biases the outcome, so the estimate cannot be
   trusted
C. Adjust the analysis for the imbalance
D. Rerun with more traffic
Reasoning: Under correct randomization, a two-percent imbalance at any realistic sample size is
astronomically unlikely. Its presence means something is differentially affecting which units are
assigned or recorded, and whatever that mechanism is will generally also affect the metric.
Statistical adjustment does not fix an unknown selection process. Answer: B.

## Metrics Design

Metrics determine what an experiment can conclude, and their design is where most of the thinking
belongs. A primary metric captures the intended effect and is decided before the experiment runs.
Guardrail metrics capture harms the change might cause and are monitored regardless of the primary
result. Diagnostic metrics explain mechanism and support interpretation without themselves being
decision criteria.

Sensitivity and validity pull in different directions. A metric close to the change — clicks on the
modified element — moves reliably and may not reflect any real benefit. A metric close to the
business outcome — retention, revenue, lifetime value — is what matters and is too noisy and too
slow to detect realistic effects. The standard resolution is a surrogate that is both sensitive and
validated against the long-term outcome, and validating the surrogate is real work that is often
skipped, leaving a metric that is trusted because it moves rather than because it predicts.

Metric definition details matter more than they appear to. Whether a ratio is computed per user or
across all events changes what it measures and how its variance behaves. Whether users with zero
activity are included changes the denominator and can reverse a result. Whether outliers are capped
changes both the estimate and its variance substantially for heavy-tailed metrics, and choosing the
cap after seeing the data is a form of the selection problem the experiment was meant to avoid.

  diagnostic metrics   mechanism and interpretation, not decisions

  surrogate metrics    sensitive and validated against the long-term outcome

## Variance Reduction and Power

An experiment's ability to detect an effect depends on the effect size, the metric's variance, and
the sample size. Sample size is often fixed by traffic and effect size by reality, so variance
reduction is the available lever, and it can be worth more than a multiple of traffic.

Regression adjustment using pre-experiment data is the standard technique. A user's metric value
before the experiment predicts their value during it, so subtracting the predicted portion removes
variance attributable to persistent user differences while leaving the treatment effect intact. The
reduction is substantial for metrics with high between-user variance and persistent behaviour, which
describes most engagement metrics, and it commonly reduces required sample size by half or more.

Stratification and blocking achieve related gains by ensuring balance on known predictive
covariates rather than relying on randomization to produce it. Trigger analysis restricts the
population to units that could actually be affected — users who reached the modified surface —
which removes the diluting effect of unaffected units and can multiply sensitivity, at the cost of a
narrower population to which the estimate applies. The trigger condition must be defined identically
in both arms, and defining it by an event that only occurs in treatment is a common and invalidating
mistake.

  the three factors    effect size, variance, sample size

  the requirement      pre-period data and a persistent metric

  the dilution removed  unaffected units contributing only noise

  the underpowered experiment  the most common design failure, and its result is
                       uninformative rather than negative
  the interpretation error  reading a non-significant underpowered result as
                       evidence of no effect

## Sequential Testing, Interference, and Special Designs

Fixed-horizon tests assume the sample size is set in advance and the result examined once. Repeatedly
checking and stopping when significance appears inflates the false positive rate dramatically —
continuous monitoring of a fixed-horizon test can reach a false positive rate several times the
nominal level. Since experimenters will look at running experiments regardless, the practical answer
is a method that permits it.

Sequential methods provide validity under continuous monitoring by adjusting the boundary over time.
Always-valid confidence sequences maintain coverage at every point, group sequential designs specify
interim analysis points with adjusted thresholds, and both trade some power at the fixed horizon for
the ability to stop early. The tradeoff is nearly always worth it in practice, because the ability
to stop a harmful experiment quickly has real value.

Interference violates the assumption that one unit's treatment does not affect another's outcome,
and it appears whenever units interact — social networks, marketplaces, shared resources, and any
system with a supply side. A marketplace experiment where treatment users book more inventory leaves
less for control users, so the measured difference overstates the effect that would occur if
everyone were treated. Cluster randomization, switchback designs alternating treatment over time
across the whole system, and explicit two-sided designs are the responses, and each measures
something subtly different from the naive comparison.

  interference         one unit's treatment affecting another's outcome

                       design that does not support the interpretation

## Quasi-Experiments and Organizational Practice

Not everything can be randomized. Changes affecting all users simultaneously, decisions already
made, and interventions at the level of a whole market require methods that approximate randomization
from observational data. Difference-in-differences compares changes across treated and untreated
groups and depends on the assumption that they would have moved in parallel absent the treatment,
which should be checked in the pre-period rather than asserted. Synthetic control constructs a
weighted combination of untreated units matching the treated one's pre-period trajectory, which is
more credible for a single treated unit than any single comparison.

Regression discontinuity exploits an arbitrary threshold — a score cutoff, a date, a size boundary —
where units just above and just below are comparable in everything except treatment. Interrupted
time series compares before and after with an explicit model of the counterfactual trend. Each of
these carries assumptions that can fail, and the credible applications are those that argue for the
assumptions with evidence rather than asserting them in a methods section.

The organizational practice around experimentation determines whether it produces knowledge or
justification. A culture that runs experiments to validate decisions already made will find the
results it wants through metric selection, timing, and segment slicing. The practices that prevent
this are pre-registration of the primary metric and the decision rule, a shared platform that makes
the analysis path uniform, review of results by someone without a stake, and — most importantly —
publishing negative results as prominently as positive ones, because a body of evidence in which
only successes are recorded teaches nothing about the base rate of success.
