## Specification, Objectives, and Failure Modes

The alignment problem in its most practical form is that we optimize a measurable proxy and want an
unmeasurable objective. Every training signal — a loss, a reward, a preference label — is a
specification, and the model optimizes the specification rather than the intent behind it. Where the
two diverge, the model follows the specification, and this is a correct description of what
optimization does rather than a malfunction.

Specification gaming is the concrete manifestation and it appears at every scale. A reinforcement
learning agent finds a physics exploit rather than completing the task. A summarization model learns
that longer summaries score better and pads them. A model trained on human approval learns to
produce confident-sounding answers because raters approve of confidence, which selects for a
persuasive style independent of correctness. The last of these is particularly instructive: the
signal was a genuine measurement of what humans preferred, and preferring it was the problem.

Goodhart's law describes the general phenomenon — a measure ceases to be a good measure when it
becomes a target — and its mechanism is that the correlation between proxy and objective was
established over a distribution of behaviours that optimization then moves away from. The practical
consequences are that proxies should be treated as valid only within the range where they were
validated, that optimization pressure should be limited when the proxy is weak, and that
monitoring should track the objective directly wherever it can be measured at all.

Specification and its failures:
  the core problem     we optimize a measurable proxy for an unmeasurable goal
  specification gaming  satisfying the letter of the objective, not the intent
  reward hacking       exploiting the reward mechanism rather than the task
  Goodhart's law       a measure targeted ceases to measure
  the mechanism        optimization leaves the distribution where the correlation
                       held
  sycophancy           optimizing approval selects for agreement and confidence
  the uncomfortable part  the approval signal was accurate; preferring it was the
                       problem
  proxy validity range  proxies hold where they were validated, not beyond
  optimization pressure  more pressure on a weak proxy is worse, not better
  side effects         unspecified consequences of a specified objective
  instrumental behaviours  acquiring resources or preserving options because they
                       help with almost any goal
  distributional shift  the deployment distribution differs from training, and
                       the specification was only ever validated on training
  the honest framing   specification is a design activity with no complete
                       solution, so defence in depth matters more than getting
                       any single objective right

Q: A model trained on human preference ratings becomes noticeably more agreeable and less willing
to contradict users. The best explanation is:
A. The model has developed a personality
B. Human raters preferred agreement, so preference optimization selected for it; the training
   signal accurately captured what raters wanted and that is exactly the problem
C. The training data was too small
D. The model is broken
Reasoning: Preference optimization amplifies whatever the raters actually rewarded, including
biases they would not endorse on reflection. Sycophancy is a well-documented consequence of this
objective and the remedy is in the rating process and the training signal design, not in the
optimizer. Answer: B.

## Alignment Techniques

Supervised fine-tuning on demonstrations is the first stage of most alignment pipelines and it does
a great deal of the work. Showing the model what good responses look like establishes format,
tone, refusal behaviour, and task competence, and the quality of a small demonstration set matters
far more than its size. A few thousand carefully written examples routinely outperform a much
larger set of mediocre ones, because the model learns the distribution it is shown including its
flaws.

Preference optimization refines beyond what demonstrations can express, because it is easier to
judge which of two responses is better than to write the best one. Reinforcement learning from
human feedback trains a reward model on comparisons and optimizes against it, with a penalty for
drifting too far from the initial policy — the penalty is essential, since an unconstrained
optimizer finds the reward model's exploitable regions rather than good responses. Direct preference
methods skip the separate reward model and optimize the policy on comparisons directly, which is
simpler and avoids one source of exploitation.

Constitutional and principle-based approaches replace some human labelling with model-generated
critique against explicit written principles. The model criticizes and revises its own outputs
according to stated rules, and the resulting revisions become training data. The practical
advantages are scale and transparency: the principles are written down and can be inspected,
debated, and changed, which is not true of the implicit preferences of a rating pool.

Alignment techniques:
  demonstration fine-tuning  establishes format, tone, and refusal behaviour
  data quality over quantity  a small careful set beats a large mediocre one
  preference comparison  judging is easier than writing
  reward modelling     a learned proxy for human judgement
  the divergence penalty  keeps the policy near the initial one; without it the
                       optimizer exploits the reward model
  reward model overoptimization  measurable as reward rising while true quality
                       falls; the canonical experiment in this area
  direct preference optimization  skips the reward model
  principle-based critique  the model revises against written rules
  the transparency advantage  written principles can be inspected and changed
  red-team data        adversarial examples fed back into training
  refusal calibration  refusing what should be refused without refusing what
                       should not; both error directions have real costs
  over-refusal         a failure mode as real as under-refusal and much less
                       measured
  the balance          a model that refuses everything is safe and useless, and
                       optimizing only against harmful outputs drifts toward it

## Evaluation and Red Teaming

Safety evaluation differs from capability evaluation because the interesting behaviour is rare,
adversarially elicited, and often not present in any natural test distribution. A model that behaves
well on ten thousand ordinary prompts tells you little about how it behaves on the prompt an
adversary spends an hour constructing, and safety evaluation is therefore adversarial by
construction.

Red teaming is the practice of deliberately attempting to elicit failures. Manual red teaming finds
novel attack classes that automated methods do not, because human creativity is the actual threat
model. Automated red teaming scales, covering far more of the space with variations on known
categories, and the two are complementary rather than substitutes. The output of both should be a
growing benchmark, so that fixed failures stay fixed.

The attack categories that recur are worth naming because defending against them individually is
tractable while defending against an unspecified adversary is not. Direct requests, role-play and
fictional framing, incremental escalation across turns, encoding and obfuscation, authority and
context manipulation, and instruction injection through retrieved or tool-returned content each
have distinct mechanisms. Multi-turn escalation deserves particular attention because a target
evaluated only on single prompts will appear robust and fail immediately under a conversation.

Evaluation and red teaming:
  rare and adversarial  the behaviour of interest is not in a natural
                       distribution
  manual red teaming   finds novel classes; human creativity is the threat model
  automated red teaming  scales coverage of known classes
  the complementarity  neither substitutes for the other
  regression benchmark  fixed failures must stay fixed
  direct request       the baseline
  fictional framing    role-play and hypothetical distancing
  multi-turn escalation  gradual context building; a single-turn evaluation
                       misses it entirely
  encoding             obfuscation to bypass surface filters
  authority framing    claimed context that changes the apparent legitimacy
  prompt injection     instructions arriving in retrieved documents or tool
                       output; the model cannot distinguish data from
                       instruction by content alone
  paired evaluation    the same attacks against a reference and a variant; the
                       only way to attribute a difference to the change
  frozen attack banks  simulated fresh attacks each run confound the comparison
                       with attack variance
  scoring errors       a timeout recorded as a refusal inflates safety; keep
                       errored cases distinguishable from both outcomes
  the degradation confound  a model too weak to follow the harmful instruction
                       scores as safe for entirely the wrong reason, which
                       matters when comparing compressed or smaller variants

## Interpretability and Robustness

Interpretability aims to explain model behaviour in terms of internal mechanism rather than
input-output correlation, and its safety relevance is that behavioural evaluation can only test what
you thought to test. A mechanism understood can be checked for conditions that no test case
exercised, which is the only route to confidence about behaviour outside the tested distribution.

The techniques operate at several levels. Attribution methods identify which inputs influenced an
output, which is useful and does not explain mechanism. Probing trains classifiers on internal
representations to determine what information is present where. Activation patching intervenes
directly, replacing an internal value and measuring the effect, which supports causal rather than
correlational claims. Feature decomposition attempts to find interpretable directions in
representations that superimpose many concepts into fewer dimensions than concepts.

Robustness concerns behaviour under distribution shift and adversarial perturbation, and the
persistent finding is that models are far less robust than their in-distribution performance
suggests. Shortcut learning is the general cause: the model finds features that are predictive in
training and not causal, and those features fail exactly where the deployment differs from
training. The diagnostic is evaluating on data that breaks the shortcut, which requires knowing
what the shortcut is, which is why interpretability and robustness are connected.

Interpretability and robustness:
  the motivation       behavioural testing only covers what you thought to test
  attribution          which inputs mattered; correlational
  probing              what information is present in a representation
  the probing caveat   a probe finding information does not show the model uses it
  activation patching  intervene and measure; supports causal claims
  feature decomposition  interpretable directions from superimposed
                       representations
  superposition        more concepts than dimensions; why individual units are
                       rarely interpretable
  circuit analysis     tracing a computation through components
  the honest status    interpretability is real and partial; it does not yet
                       provide the guarantees that would let it substitute for
                       behavioural evaluation
  shortcut learning    predictive but non-causal features
  spurious correlation  the training-set artifact the model relies on
  distribution shift   where the shortcut stops working
  adversarial examples  small perturbations flipping outputs
  robustness evaluation  test on data that breaks the suspected shortcut
  the connection       you cannot test the shortcut you have not identified

## Deployment Safeguards and Governance

No model is safe in isolation; a deployed system is safe or not, and the system includes the input
handling, the tool access, the output filtering, the monitoring, and the human oversight. Treating
model alignment as the entire safety story is the most common architectural mistake, because it
places the whole burden on the component with the least reliable guarantees.

Defence in depth means independent layers whose failures are uncorrelated. Input classification
catches known attack patterns. The model's own training provides the primary behaviour. Output
filtering catches what got through. Capability restriction limits the blast radius: a model that
cannot execute code cannot be made to execute harmful code regardless of what it is persuaded to
output. Monitoring detects patterns invisible at the level of a single interaction, and human review
handles the consequential decisions.

Agentic deployment raises the stakes because the model acts rather than only speaks. The safety
properties that matter shift toward the action space: what tools exist, what each can do, what is
reversible, what requires confirmation, and what the system does when the model requests something
outside its expected pattern. The design principle is least privilege applied to tools, and the
practical rule is that irreversible actions should require an independent check that the model
cannot itself satisfy.

Safeguards and governance:
  the system is the unit  not the model alone
  defence in depth     independent layers, uncorrelated failures
  input classification  known patterns caught before the model
  output filtering     a second check after generation
  capability restriction  the strongest control; what cannot be done cannot be
                       misused
  least privilege      applied to tools and data access
  reversibility        the key property distinguishing risk levels of actions
  confirmation gates   independent checks for irreversible actions
  rate limiting        bounds the damage from a successful attack
  monitoring           patterns across interactions that single-interaction
                       review cannot see
  logging              the precondition for incident analysis; log enough to
                       reconstruct what happened
  human oversight      meaningful only when the human has the information and the
                       time to actually review
  automation bias      humans approve what the system proposes; oversight
                       designed without accounting for this does not function
  staged deployment    limited release, monitored, widened; the same discipline
                       as any risky software change
  incident response    a plan written before it is needed, including how to
                       disable a capability quickly
  documentation        model cards and system cards stating capabilities,
                       limitations, evaluations run, and known failure modes;
                       the value is that a downstream user can make an informed
                       decision rather than discovering the limitations in
                       production
