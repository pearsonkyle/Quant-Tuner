## Audio Representation

Audio machine learning begins with representation, and the choice determines what the model can
learn and how efficiently. A raw waveform at 16 kilohertz is 16,000 samples per second, so a
ten-second clip is 160,000 values — a far longer sequence than a comparable amount of text, which
is the structural difficulty of the domain.

The spectrogram is the standard representation and converts the waveform into a time-frequency
image by taking the Fourier transform over successive overlapping windows. The window length sets
the time-frequency tradeoff directly: a short window localizes events in time and resolves
frequency poorly, and a long window does the reverse. Speech typically uses windows of twenty to
twenty-five milliseconds with ten-millisecond hops, which is chosen because the vocal tract's
configuration is approximately stationary over that duration.

The mel scale reflects human pitch perception, which is approximately logarithmic above a few
hundred hertz, and warping the frequency axis to it concentrates resolution where the auditory
system has it. A mel spectrogram with sixty-four to a hundred and twenty-eight bands is the
standard input to speech and audio models, reducing the dimensionality substantially while
preserving the perceptually relevant structure.

                       requires twice the highest frequency of interest
  bit depth            16-bit is standard for delivery; higher for processing
                       headroom

  spectrogram          time-frequency; the standard representation
  window length        the time-frequency tradeoff; 20-25 ms for speech
  hop length           the frame advance; 10 ms is standard

                       models prefer the correlated mel representation

                       therefore requires estimating it, which is why vocoders
                       exist
  constant-Q transform  logarithmic frequency spacing; suits music where pitch

The discarding of phase deserves emphasis because it creates the central difficulty in audio
generation. A magnitude spectrogram is a convenient and lossy representation — the phase carries
information about the fine temporal structure, and reconstructing audio from magnitude alone
requires estimating it. Classical iterative methods produce audible artifacts, and neural vocoders
that learn the mapping from spectrogram to waveform are what made high-quality synthesis
practical. Any pipeline that generates a spectrogram must address this, and it is the reason
end-to-end waveform models became attractive despite their sequence length.

Q: A speech model uses a 25 ms analysis window. Doubling it to 50 ms would:
A. Improve both time and frequency resolution
B. Improve frequency resolution and degrade time resolution, and would also span multiple
   phonetic events, violating the stationarity assumption
C. Have no effect
D. Improve time resolution
Reasoning: The time-frequency uncertainty relation means a longer window resolves frequency more
finely and localizes events more coarsely. For speech, 20-25 ms is chosen because the vocal tract
configuration is roughly stationary over that interval; a 50 ms window spans a transition between
phonemes and smears them together. Answer: B.

## Speech Recognition

Automatic speech recognition converts audio to text, and the architecture has shifted
substantially. Classical systems combined an acoustic model mapping audio to phonetic units, a
pronunciation lexicon mapping words to phone sequences, and a language model scoring word
sequences, with a decoder searching over the combination. Each component was trained separately
and the pipeline required linguistic resources.

End-to-end models map audio directly to text, learning the acoustic, lexical, and linguistic
relationships jointly. Connectionist temporal classification handles the alignment problem — the
audio and the transcript have different lengths with no given correspondence — by summing over all
alignments, which permits training without frame-level labels. Attention-based encoder-decoder
models learn the alignment implicitly. Transducer models combine an audio encoder with a
prediction network, which permits streaming because the output can be produced incrementally.

The streaming requirement divides architectures sharply. An offline model can attend to the entire
utterance, including audio after the current position, which improves accuracy substantially. A
streaming model must produce output with bounded latency and can therefore attend only to past and
a limited amount of future context. The accuracy difference is real, which is why systems
frequently run a streaming model for immediate display and rescore with an offline model when the
utterance completes.

Speech recognition:
  acoustic model       audio to phonetic units; classical pipeline component
  pronunciation lexicon  words to phone sequences; requires linguistic resources
  language model       scores word sequences; still valuable for domain adaptation
                       even with end-to-end acoustic modelling
  CTC                  sums over alignments; permits training without frame labels
  attention encoder-decoder  learns alignment implicitly; strong offline
  transducer           encoder plus prediction network; the standard streaming
                       architecture
  streaming            bounded latency, limited future context; measurably less
                       accurate than offline
  two-pass             stream for display, rescore offline for the final result
  beam search          decoding; wider beams improve accuracy at latency cost
  shallow fusion       combine an external language model at decoding; the
                       practical route to domain adaptation
  self-supervised pretraining  learn representations from unlabelled audio, then
                       fine-tune on a small labelled set; the change that made
                       low-resource recognition feasible
  word error rate      the standard metric; substitutions plus deletions plus
                       insertions over reference words
  the failure modes    accented speech, code-switching, overlapping speakers,
                       domain vocabulary, and noise; performance varies far more
                       across these than aggregate figures suggest

Self-supervised pretraining deserves emphasis because it changed the resource requirements. A
model pretrained on large quantities of unlabelled audio — learning to predict masked portions or
to distinguish true from distractor continuations — acquires representations capturing phonetic
structure without any transcripts. Fine-tuning on a small labelled set then reaches accuracy that
previously required orders of magnitude more transcribed data. This made recognition practical for
languages and domains where transcription is expensive, which is most of them.

The variation in performance across speaker populations deserves stating because aggregate word
error rates conceal it. Systems trained predominantly on one accent, dialect, or demographic
perform substantially worse on others, with documented disparities of a factor of two or more in
error rate between speaker groups. This is a data composition problem rather than a modelling one,
and reporting per-group performance rather than an aggregate is what makes it visible.

## Speaker and Audio Understanding

Speaker recognition determines who is speaking, and it divides into verification — confirming a
claimed identity — and identification — determining which of a set of known speakers. The modern
approach embeds an utterance into a fixed-dimensional vector such that utterances from the same
speaker are close, and comparison is then a distance computation. This permits enrolment with a
short sample and comparison against a large set efficiently.

Speaker diarization determines who spoke when, which is required for any multi-speaker recording.
The classical pipeline segments the audio, embeds each segment, clusters the embeddings, and
assigns labels. Its persistent difficulty is overlapping speech, where two people talk
simultaneously — common in natural conversation and handled poorly by approaches assuming one
speaker per segment. End-to-end methods that predict per-speaker activity directly handle overlap
by construction.

General audio understanding covers classification and detection of non-speech sound — recognizing
a dog bark, a glass break, a machine fault, a bird species. The techniques parallel image
classification with spectrograms as the input, and the distinctive difficulties are that events
overlap, that the relevant timescales vary from milliseconds to minutes, and that labelled data is
scarce relative to images.

Speaker and audio understanding:
  speaker verification  confirm a claimed identity; a threshold on similarity
  speaker identification  which of a known set
  speaker embedding    a fixed vector per utterance; comparison by distance
  enrolment            a short reference sample per speaker
  diarization          who spoke when
  overlapping speech   common in conversation, handled poorly by segment-based
                       approaches
  target speaker extraction  isolate one speaker given a reference sample
  source separation    separate mixed sources; the cocktail party problem
  permutation invariant training  the separation output order is arbitrary, so
                       the loss must consider all assignments
  voice activity detection  speech versus not; the front end of most pipelines,
                       and its errors propagate through everything after it
  keyword spotting     detect specific words continuously at very low power;
                       always-on wake words
  audio event detection  what happened and when
  audio tagging        what is present, without timing
  acoustic scene       the environment; street, office, forest
  anti-spoofing        detect replayed or synthesized audio; increasingly
                       necessary as synthesis quality rises

Anti-spoofing deserves attention because synthesis quality has made voice authentication
substantially less secure. A few seconds of reference audio now suffices to produce speech
convincing to a human listener and to many verification systems, which means voice alone is no
longer adequate authentication for anything consequential. Detection systems exist and are in an
adversarial contest with synthesis, and the practical response is treating voice as one factor
among several rather than as sufficient in itself.

## Speech Synthesis

Text to speech has progressed through distinct generations with substantially different quality.
Concatenative synthesis assembles recorded units, which gives natural segments and audible
discontinuities at the joins and requires a large recorded corpus per voice. Parametric synthesis
generates from a model of the vocal tract, which is compact and flexible and sounds buzzy. Neural
synthesis learns the mapping end to end and reaches quality that listeners rate comparably to
recorded speech.

The modern architecture is typically two stages. An acoustic model maps text — or phonemes derived
from it — to a mel spectrogram, handling pronunciation, prosody, and timing. A vocoder converts the
spectrogram to a waveform, solving the phase reconstruction problem. Separating them permits each
to be trained and improved independently, and the vocoder is reusable across voices.

Prosody is where synthesis remains weakest and where the remaining gap to human speech lies.
Segmental quality — whether the phonemes sound right — is essentially solved. Prosody covers the
rhythm, stress, intonation, and emphasis that convey meaning beyond the words, and it is
underdetermined by the text: the same sentence can be spoken many ways, all correct, conveying
different emphasis and attitude. A model trained on text-to-audio pairs learns an average prosody,
which is why synthesized speech sounds competent and slightly flat.

Speech synthesis:
  concatenative        recorded units; natural segments, audible joins
  parametric           vocal tract model; compact, buzzy
  neural end to end    the current approach; near-human segmental quality
  two-stage            acoustic model to spectrogram, vocoder to waveform
  vocoder              spectrogram to waveform; solves phase reconstruction
  autoregressive vocoder  highest quality, slow, sample by sample
  parallel vocoder     generates the whole waveform at once; fast, and the quality
                       gap has largely closed
  text normalization   numbers, dates, abbreviations, and symbols to spoken form;
                       unglamorous and a major source of errors
  grapheme to phoneme  pronunciation; ambiguous for homographs, requiring context
  prosody              rhythm, stress, intonation; underdetermined by the text and
                       where the remaining gap lies
  duration modelling   how long each phoneme lasts; determines the rhythm
  voice cloning        a few seconds of reference; raises consent and misuse
                       questions that the technical capability does not address
  emotional control    conditioning on a style reference or an explicit label
  streaming synthesis  produce audio before the full text is processed; required
                       for conversational latency

Text normalization deserves mention because it is unglamorous and is where a substantial share of
synthesis errors originate. Converting written text to what should be spoken requires expanding
numbers according to context — a year, a quantity, a phone number, and a decimal are all read
differently — expanding abbreviations, handling currency and units, and disambiguating homographs
from context. These are largely rule-based and language-specific, they interact with the
pronunciation model, and a system with excellent acoustic quality that reads a date incorrectly is
judged on the error rather than on the quality.

[assistant] The model was trained on clean close-talk speech and deployed at two metres in

Distance is the largest. At two metres rather than thirty centimetres, the direct sound is roughly
sixteen times weaker while the reverberant field is unchanged, so the ratio of direct to reverberant
energy falls sharply. Reverberation smears the temporal structure that the model relies on —
each phoneme's energy persists into the following ones — and your field reverberation time is
double your office's.

Background noise at 12 dB signal-to-noise is a substantial degradation from 35 dB, and the model
has never seen anything like it.

Four responses, and the first two require no new data collection.

Augment the training data with simulated far-field conditions. Convolve your clean training audio
with measured or simulated room impulse responses spanning realistic reverberation times, and add
noise at a range of signal-to-noise ratios. This is the highest-value change and it is
straightforward — impulse response datasets are available, and the augmentation is applied at
training time. Expect a substantial fraction of the gap to close.

Add a multichannel front end if the hardware has more than one microphone. Beamforming steers
sensitivity toward the speaker and away from noise sources, typically gaining several decibels of
effective signal-to-noise ratio, and it also reduces the reverberant contribution. Even two
microphones help meaningfully.

Then fine-tune on real field audio once you have some. Simulated augmentation covers the acoustics
and does not capture the actual devices, rooms, and speaking styles, and a modest amount of real
in-domain data typically outperforms a large amount of simulated.

One thing to check first: verify your voice activity detection is not the problem. If it is
triggering late or cutting off early in noise, the recognizer receives truncated audio and the
word error rate rises for reasons unrelated to the acoustic model. Inspect a sample of field
recordings with the detected boundaries marked, because this failure is common and is fixed
differently from everything above.

## Music, Evaluation, and Deployment

Music information retrieval has its own tasks and its own structure. Pitch and note detection
extract the musical content. Beat and tempo tracking find the rhythmic structure. Chord recognition
identifies harmony. Source separation isolates instruments or vocals, which has become good enough
to be used commercially for remixing and practice tools. Genre and mood classification categorize.
And generation produces new material, which raises the same rights questions as generative models
in other media.

The distinctive property of music is that the relevant structure is hierarchical and spans
timescales from milliseconds to minutes. A model attending only locally captures timbre and misses
form; capturing structure over minutes requires either very long context or an explicit
hierarchical representation. This is why music generation produces convincing short segments more
readily than coherent long pieces.

Evaluation in audio is difficult because the target is perceptual. Objective metrics correlate
imperfectly with human judgment, and the standard for synthesis quality remains a subjective
listening test with a substantial number of listeners. Automated metrics are used for iteration and
the final judgment is human, which is expensive and is why the field moves more slowly than
domains with reliable automatic evaluation.

  pitch detection      fundamental frequency; polyphonic is far harder than

  chord recognition    harmonic content over time

  music generation     convincing short segments, coherent long form remains hard

The wake word constraint deserves the closing note because it is an unusual engineering problem.
The model runs continuously on battery, so its power budget is measured in milliwatts, which
constrains it to a few tens of kilobytes and a fraction of a processor core. It must have a very
low false accept rate, since a false accept means the device begins recording and transmitting
audio the user did not intend — a privacy incident rather than an inconvenience. And it must have
a low false reject rate, since a device that does not respond is perceived as broken. Meeting all
three simultaneously is what makes wake word detection a specialized problem rather than a small
instance of keyword spotting, and it is typically solved with a cascade where a tiny always-on
model gates a larger verification model that runs only on a candidate detection.
