## Generative Model Families

Generative models learn a distribution over data and sample new instances from it. Several
families have dominated in succession, and each made a different tradeoff among sample
quality, diversity, training stability, and sampling speed.

Variational autoencoders encode data into a latent distribution and decode samples back,
trained by maximizing a lower bound on the likelihood. They train stably, provide a
meaningful and smoothly interpolable latent space, and produce characteristically blurry
samples — a direct consequence of the reconstruction term, typically a pixel-wise
likelihood, which is minimized by predicting the average of plausible outputs rather than
committing to one.

Generative adversarial networks pit a generator against a discriminator in a minimax game.
When they work they produce extremely sharp samples, and the difficulty is that they
frequently do not work: training is a two-player game with no single objective decreasing
monotonically, so there is no loss curve that indicates progress. Mode collapse — the
generator producing a narrow subset of the distribution because that subset reliably fools
the discriminator — is the characteristic failure, and a decade of architectural and
regularization work went into mitigating it.

Diffusion models learn to reverse a gradual noising process, and they now dominate image
generation. Training is a simple, stable regression objective: given a noised image and a
noise level, predict the noise that was added. There is no adversarial game, no mode
collapse, and coverage of the distribution is excellent. The cost is sampling speed, since
generation requires many sequential denoising steps rather than a single forward pass, and
a substantial fraction of research effort has gone into reducing the step count.

Model families compared:
  VAE                  stable training, smooth latent space, blurry samples;
                       now used mainly as the encoder/decoder inside latent
                       diffusion rather than as a generator
  GAN                  sharp samples, fast single-pass generation, unstable
                       training, mode collapse; still competitive for
                       real-time and for super-resolution
  normalizing flow     exact likelihood, invertible, architecturally constrained
  autoregressive       exact likelihood, excellent quality, slow sequential
                       sampling; dominant for text, competitive for images at
                       scale
  diffusion            stable training, excellent quality and coverage, slow
                       multi-step sampling; the current default for images
  consistency / distilled  diffusion distilled into few or one step; trades some
                       quality for large speed gains

The convergence worth noting is that the boundaries have blurred. Latent diffusion uses a
VAE to compress images into a latent space where diffusion operates, combining the VAE's
efficient representation with diffusion's sample quality. Diffusion transformers replace
the convolutional U-Net backbone with a transformer, importing the scaling behaviour that
worked for language. Consistency models distill a multi-step diffusion sampler into a
one-step generator, which is functionally a GAN-quality generator trained without an
adversary.

## Diffusion Mechanics

The forward process progressively adds Gaussian noise to an image over a schedule of
timesteps, until after enough steps the result is indistinguishable from pure noise. This
process is fixed rather than learned, and it has a convenient property: because the sum of
Gaussians is Gaussian, the image at any timestep can be sampled directly from the original
in one step, without simulating the intermediate steps. This is what makes training
efficient — each training example samples a random timestep, adds the corresponding noise,
and trains the network to predict it.

The reverse process is what the model learns. Given a noised image and its noise level,
the network predicts the noise component, which is equivalent to predicting the direction
back toward the data manifold. Sampling starts from pure noise and iteratively removes
predicted noise, gradually revealing structure. The intuition worth carrying is that early
steps, at high noise, determine global composition and layout, while late steps, at low
noise, determine fine texture and detail. This is why changing the seed alters composition
entirely while changing the last few steps alters only surface detail.

The noise schedule determines how noise is distributed across timesteps and has a
substantial effect on quality. A linear schedule spends many steps in regimes that carry
little information; cosine schedules distribute the signal-to-noise ratio more usefully.
The schedule also interacts with resolution: higher-resolution images retain more
recognizable structure at a given noise level, because noise averages out over more
pixels, which is why schedules tuned at one resolution produce undertrained global
structure at another and why resolution-dependent schedule shifts are applied in newer
models.

Diffusion concepts:
  forward process        fixed Gaussian noising; closed-form at any timestep
  reverse process        learned denoising; the model predicts the noise
  prediction target      epsilon (noise), x0 (clean image), or v (velocity);
                         v-prediction behaves better at the schedule extremes
  noise schedule         linear, cosine, or sigmoid; controls signal-to-noise
                         across timesteps
  timestep embedding     the network is conditioned on the noise level; without
                         it the model cannot know how much to remove
  classifier-free guidance  the standard conditioning mechanism; see below
  latent diffusion       operate in a compressed VAE latent rather than pixels;
                         roughly 8x downsampling gives ~64x fewer elements
  U-Net vs DiT           convolutional backbone with skip connections, versus a
                         transformer operating on latent patches

Latent diffusion is the change that made high-resolution generation practical on consumer
hardware. Running diffusion directly on 1024-by-1024 pixels is enormously expensive;
compressing to a 128-by-128 latent with a learned autoencoder reduces the element count by
about sixty-four while preserving the perceptually relevant information, since the
autoencoder is trained to discard exactly the high-frequency detail that a perceptual loss
deems unimportant. The visible cost is that the autoencoder imposes a ceiling on fine
detail — very small text and fine regular patterns are where latent-space artifacts
appear, because those are precisely what the compression discards.

Q: Changing only the last five denoising steps of a fifty-step generation produces:
A. A completely different composition
B. Minor changes to fine detail and texture
C. No change at all
D. A different subject entirely
Reasoning: Global structure is determined at high noise levels early in sampling, when the
image is mostly noise and the model is deciding broad layout. By the final steps the
composition is fixed and only fine detail remains to be resolved. This is why refinement
passes and detail-focused upscalers operate on the low-noise end. Answer: B.

## Conditioning, Guidance, and Control

Text conditioning works by encoding a prompt with a text encoder and injecting those
embeddings into the denoising network through cross-attention. Which text encoder is used
matters considerably: contrastive image-text encoders capture visual concepts well and
handle compositional and relational language poorly, which is why early text-to-image
models struggled with counts, spatial relations, and attribute binding. Adding a large
language model as an additional or replacement encoder improved prompt adherence markedly,
because such models represent syntax and relations that a contrastive encoder trained on
short captions never learned.

Classifier-free guidance is the mechanism that makes prompts actually work. The model is
trained both conditionally and unconditionally by randomly dropping the conditioning during
training. At sampling time both predictions are computed and extrapolated away from the
unconditional toward the conditional, amplifying the influence of the prompt. The guidance
scale controls the amount, and its effect is a clear tradeoff: low values give diverse,
loosely-related images, high values give strong prompt adherence with oversaturated colours,
harsh contrast, and reduced diversity. Typical usable ranges sit between five and nine for
most models, with distilled and rectified-flow models often preferring much lower values.

Structural control conditions generation on spatial information rather than on text. An
auxiliary network takes a control image — edges, depth, human pose, segmentation — and
injects features into the denoising network, so the output follows the specified structure
while the prompt determines content and style. This is what converts text-to-image from a
slot machine into a directable tool, because it separates the decisions an artist wants to
make explicitly from the ones they are happy to delegate.

Control mechanisms:
  text prompt            content and style; the coarsest and most convenient handle
  negative prompt        conditioning the unconditional branch; steers away from
                         described attributes
  guidance scale         prompt adherence versus diversity and naturalness
  seed                   determines the initial noise, hence composition; fix it
                         to isolate the effect of any other change
  image-to-image         start from a noised version of an input rather than pure
                         noise; the denoising strength sets how much is retained
  inpainting             regenerate a masked region conditioned on the surround
  outpainting            extend beyond the original frame
  structural control     edge, depth, pose, or segmentation conditioning
  IP-adapter / reference conditioning on an image's style or subject rather than
                         its structure
  regional prompting     different prompts applied to different image regions

Image-to-image denoising strength is the parameter most worth understanding precisely. It
determines how far back into the noise schedule the input is pushed before denoising
begins. At low strength only a few steps run, so the output closely resembles the input and
the prompt has little influence. At high strength the input is nearly destroyed and the
result is effectively a fresh generation. The useful working range for guided variation is
usually between 0.3 and 0.6, and the parameter interacts with step count — because the
steps actually executed are the total multiplied by the strength, a low strength with few
total steps may run only two or three denoising steps and produce artifacts.

Samplers solve the reverse diffusion differential equation and differ in accuracy per
step. Ancestral samplers inject fresh noise at each step, so they do not converge to a
fixed image as steps increase — more steps give a different image rather than a better one,
which is sometimes desirable and often confusing. Deterministic solvers converge, so
increasing steps refines toward a limit. Higher-order solvers achieve good quality in
fifteen to twenty-five steps where first-order methods need fifty or more. There is no
universally best sampler; the practical approach is to fix a seed and prompt, sweep
samplers and step counts, and choose from the grid.

Classifier-free guidance at 14 is extrapolating far past the conditional prediction, which
pushes latent values outside the range the decoder was trained on. That clips highlights,
saturates colour, and collapses diversity — exactly your three symptoms. For older
diffusion models 7 to 9 was reasonable; for a distilled flow-matching model the usable
range is roughly 1.5 to 3.5, because the distillation already internalized much of the
guidance effect. Try 2.5 first.

Steps do not help because steps and guidance address different things. Steps control how
accurately the sampler integrates the trajectory; guidance controls where the trajectory
goes. An overcooked trajectory integrated more precisely is still overcooked. Worse, you
are using an ancestral sampler, which injects fresh noise every step and therefore does not
converge — at 60 steps you are adding stochastic variation rather than refining. Drop to
20-28 steps, and if you want deterministic refinement behaviour, switch to a non-ancestral
solver.

If you want strong prompt adherence without the saturation, use CFG rescaling at around
0.7. It renormalizes the guided prediction back toward the statistics of the unguided one,
which keeps the adherence while removing most of the contrast blowout. Many interfaces
expose this as a separate slider and it is underused.

Order of changes so you can see what each does: fix the seed, set guidance to 2.5, then
sweep steps 16/20/28, then try CFG rescale. Changing them together will tell you nothing.

## Latent Space and Fine-Tuning

The latent space of a generative model is where the interesting structure lives.
Interpolating between two latent points produces a smooth transition through plausible
images rather than a crossfade, because the path stays on the learned manifold. Spherical
interpolation is the correct method for Gaussian latents rather than linear, since linear
interpolation passes through the low-density centre of a high-dimensional Gaussian and
produces washed-out midpoints — a specific consequence of the fact that in high dimensions
almost all the probability mass of a Gaussian lies in a thin shell.

Semantic directions exist in latent space and can be discovered. Finding the direction
that corresponds to a specific attribute — adding a smile, changing age, altering lighting
— allows controlled editing by moving along it. Directions can be found by supervised
means, averaging the difference between labelled groups, or unsupervised, through principal
component analysis of the latent distribution. This is the mechanism behind most latent
editing tools, and its limitation is entanglement: directions are rarely pure, so moving
along one attribute drags others with it.

Fine-tuning adapts a pretrained model to a specific subject, style, or domain, and the
methods differ mainly in how many parameters they touch.

Fine-tuning approaches:
  full fine-tuning     updates all weights; strongest adaptation, needs the most
                       data and compute, and risks catastrophic forgetting of
                       everything else the model knew
  LoRA                 low-rank updates to attention and linear layers; a few
                       megabytes, composable at inference, the practical default
  DreamBooth           binds a rare token to a specific subject using a handful of
                       images plus a prior-preservation loss to prevent the model
                       collapsing the whole class onto that subject
  textual inversion    learns a new token embedding only; tiny, weaker, extremely
                       portable
  hypernetworks        a small network modulating the main model's weights
  IP-adapter           conditions on a reference image at inference with no
                       training at all

The most common fine-tuning failure is overfitting to incidental features of the training
set. Training a subject on twenty photographs all taken in the same room produces a model
that reproduces the room whenever the subject is requested, because the model has no way to
know which co-occurring features are the subject and which are the background. The
remedies are diverse training images varying everything except the subject, captions that
explicitly describe the incidental features so the model attributes them elsewhere,
conservative learning rates, and stopping early — with samples checked at multiple
checkpoints rather than trusting the final one, since the best checkpoint is frequently not
the last.

## Practice, Evaluation, and Ethics

Prompt engineering for images is more empirical and less transferable than it appears.
Prompts that work well on one model often work poorly on another, because they are
exploiting the particular caption distribution the text encoder was trained on. Long lists
of quality tokens were effective on models trained on tagged aesthetic datasets and are
largely inert on models trained on descriptive natural-language captions. The durable
advice is to describe the intended image plainly, be specific about the elements that
matter, and test rather than accumulate incantations.

Reproducibility requires recording more than the prompt. The model checkpoint and its exact
version, the seed, the sampler, the step count, the guidance scale, the resolution, and any
adapters with their weights all affect the output, and a workflow that does not log them
produces images that cannot be regenerated or systematically varied. Node-based interfaces
encode the whole graph, which is one reason they have become standard for serious work
despite their steeper learning curve.

Evaluation of generative image models is genuinely unsolved. Automated metrics measure
distributional similarity between generated and real images and correlate poorly with human
judgment of individual images. Prompt-adherence metrics using image-text similarity capture
whether the right objects appear and miss composition, count, and relation. Human
evaluation remains the standard for quality and is expensive, slow, and subject to
presentation biases. For an artist the relevant evaluation is whether the system reliably
produces work they want, which no benchmark measures.

Practice and ethics:
  log every parameter    model, seed, sampler, steps, guidance, adapters,
                         resolution; otherwise the image is unreproducible
  fix the seed to compare changing two things at once teaches you nothing
  work in grids          sweep one parameter across a row; judge from the grid
  upscale separately     generate composition at native resolution, then refine;
                         generating directly at high resolution produces duplicated
                         subjects in models trained at lower resolution
  training data          most large models were trained on scraped images without
                         consent or compensation; this is an unresolved legal and
                         ethical question, not a settled one
  style imitation        prompting for a living artist's name is legally
                         contested and ethically contentious; imitating a
                         recognizable individual style is different in kind from
                         working in a historical movement
  provenance             content credentials and embedded metadata; disclosure
                         norms are still forming and disclosure is cheap
  consent for likeness   generating identifiable people raises separate issues
                         from style, with clearer legal exposure

The duplicated-subject artifact deserves mention because it confuses people and has a
specific cause. A model trained predominantly at one resolution has learned the spatial
frequency of composition at that scale; asked to generate at twice the linear size, it
produces content at the learned scale twice over, yielding two heads or two horizons. The
solution is to generate at or near the training resolution and upscale in a separate pass,
usually with a low-strength image-to-image refinement that adds detail without changing
composition. Models trained with multi-resolution or aspect-ratio bucketing suffer from
this much less, which is why native resolution support has improved across generations.

On the ethical questions, the honest position is that they are open rather than settled.
The training data of most widely used image models includes copyrighted work gathered
without permission, litigation is ongoing in multiple jurisdictions, and the outcomes will
differ by jurisdiction. Artists working with these tools face a real choice about what they
are comfortable with, and the available options — models trained on licensed or public
domain data, fine-tuning only on one's own work, disclosing use — involve genuine tradeoffs
in capability. Pretending the question is resolved in either direction misrepresents the
situation.
